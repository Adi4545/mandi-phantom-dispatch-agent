# Independent novelty and venue review

**A2 · 21 November 2026 · Source revision d20a0cb25827b73688e4ced19948ac5c01e49f6b**

**Verdict.** The strongest candidate contribution is a retrospective comparison of dispatch rules on the same Indian mandi decision episodes that makes arrival-date quote availability, costs and the assumed payoff when a quote is missing explicit. Quantifying how conclusions about routes and policies change with this assumption would be an applied empirical result. The source record establishes priority for agricultural transfer-cost analysis, AGMARKNET price sparsity, farmer destination choice, freight-aware mandi tools, abstention and risk calibration separately. It also establishes substantial overlap in combinations. Use “phantom spatial arbitrage” only as an author-defined label with a precise denominator and scenario. No reviewed evidence establishes who first used this phrase or the physical loss on any unobserved trip.

## Venue assessment

**PLOS ONE** is a candidate for an empirical decision-evaluation paper after reproducibility and validity issues are resolved. Its criteria require technically sound methods, coherent evidence-based conclusions and accessible supporting data. Its comparable Odisha brinjal study fitted models to daily AGMARKNET data and reported market-level forecast errors and arrival profiles. That paper’s inclusion establishes topical fit only. Prepare the Data Availability Statement, complete methods and input-access details before submission. **ACM COMPASS** has published Madaan et al. and welcomes work on computing, development and sustainable societies. A local user/context study would strengthen the fit. The verified 2026 paper track closed 21 March 2026. Its guide required anonymized single-column PDF, AI-use disclosure, and typically 7,000–8,000 words. Check the next call separately.
