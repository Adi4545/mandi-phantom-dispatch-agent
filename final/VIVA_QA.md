# Viva answers, in plain language

**What is the result, in one breath?** On 56,172 days, chasing a lagged portal gap above ₹100 loses money in the model 41% of the time. A rule that requires the gap, after freight, to exceed ₹200 makes a higher average than a yesterday's-price road rule at a similar dispatch rate, and a worse worst-10% (ES10). The road rule is safer. The depth of the bad tail depends on what we assume when no exact-variety price was recorded.

**What is a gradient-boosted tree?** Start with a simple tree that predicts price from a few numbers, such as how old the last quote is. It is a bad predictor. Fit a second tree to the mistakes of the first, then a third to the mistakes that remain, and add them up in small steps so no one tree dominates. That sum is a gradient-boosted tree. The older draft used one (`HistGradientBoostingRegressor` in scikit-learn). This revision does not. The decision is a subtraction and a cutoff, which you can recompute on a calculator. The pickled tree also does not open on a current scikit-learn, because it was saved with version 1.3.0.

**What is forecasting here?** Forecasting means guessing a price or a weather value that has not happened yet, using only what you already know. A portal price from yesterday is not a forecast. It is a record. ERA5 is a reconstructed weather history. It is not sitting on a farmer's phone at 6 a.m. The revision does not forecast. It uses yesterday's exact-variety modal and a fixed 20°C spoilage rate taken from a published one-day loss figure.

**What is a threshold?** A threshold is the smallest advantage you demand before you act. Example. Lagged destination modal ₹2,000, local modal ₹1,500, freight and fee ₹95. The score is \(2000 - 1500 - 95 = 405\). If the threshold is ₹200, you go. If it is ₹500, you stay. The ₹200 figure was chosen on January–May 2025 because, on a fixed list of cutoffs, it had the lowest loss rate among rules that still dispatched at least a quarter of validation episodes. It was then locked. The test months did not pick it.

**What is a missing-price sensitivity?** Sometimes the destination has no exact-variety row on the arrival day. The model has to invent a settlement. The base invention is: if another variety of the same crop was recorded, sell its minimum at a 10% discount; if nothing was recorded, pay 1.5 times the freight to turn around, plus a little spoilage. Sensitivity means repeating the whole test with that 1.5 replaced by 0, 0.5, and 1.0, and the 10% replaced by 0 and 5%, and reading the table by crop and by state. In this revision the share of losing trips hardly changes. ES10 changes by about ₹245 per quintal for the cautious rule. So the existence of losses is not an artifact of the 1.5, but the size of the worst decile is.

**What is ES10?** Line up every episode's result from the worst loss to the best gain. Take the worst tenth of that line, and if the tenth does not land on a whole episode, give the boundary episode a fractional weight. Average those. That average is ES10. Worked example: the numbers 1 through 11. Ten percent of 11 is 1.1. ES10 \(= (1 \times 1 + 0.1 \times 2) / 1.1 = 1.09\). The old tables that averaged every point below a 10% quantile are a different statistic. Do not quote them as ES10. Refusals are stored as zero, so ES10 describes the mixture of trips and stay-home days, not only the trucks that left.

**What is a bootstrap?** The test days are not independent. A mandi has many days, and a fortnight has many mandis. A bootstrap redraws whole mandis and whole fortnights with replacement, recomputes the mean gap, and repeats 2,000 times. The 95% interval is the middle 95% of those recomputed gaps. For the cautious rule minus the tuned road rule the interval is about +₹43 to +₹94 per quintal. That supports a higher mean. It says nothing good about ES10, which is worse for the cautious rule on the point estimate. A second redraw that clusters on the destination the road rule chose gives a similar mean interval. It is a sensitivity, not a second discovery.

**Why did the old H2 test fail?** The older same-day rule, compared with a same-day road rule on 69,526 episodes, was about ₹6 per quintal ahead. The interval ran from about −₹17 to +₹25. That includes zero. "Inconclusive" is the right word. This revision does not overturn that sentence. It asks a different question, with lagged quotes and a sequential settlement, and even there the road rule wins on the loss rate and on ES10.

**What about Himachal apples?** The grade-explicit holdout was not rebuilt under the lagged settlement. In the stored older run the heavier rule made about ₹605 per quintal and dispatched about 19% of lots, against about ₹1,151 and 34% for the road rule. In the new four-crop table, the non-grade apple slice has a worse ES10 for the cautious rule than for the road rule. Apples are not a success story.

**Is a missing row a closed market?** No. It means no exact-variety price was recorded. The yard may have traded another variety, failed to upload, or not traded. The model picks a penalty. The data do not show a padlock.

**Was any of this a farmer's profit?** No. There are no receipts. The exercise scores rules on a formula.
