## 1. The 1-Minute Elevator Pitch

> *"Every morning, price apps tell Indian vegetable and apple farmers that a distant mandi 200 km away pays ₹500–₹1,300/qtl more than their home yard. Across 69,526 holdout dispatch opportunities in six Indian states (Maharashtra, UP, Karnataka, MP, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh), we observe that 58.94% of naive price-chasing dispatches hit closed yards or missing variety auctions and 40.46% lose money outright—a gap we call Phantom Spatial Arbitrage. Even standard point-ML price predictors lose money on 45.28% of dispatches on the full market.*  
> *So I built `MandiGuard`: a **risk-gated dispatch decision-support system** implemented as a four-step verification cascade (`T1` market vitality → `T2` OSRM road and statutory APMC fee → `T3` Q10 weather spoilage → `T4` 80% conformal safety cushion) with an explicit `REFUSE_PHANTOM_SPREAD` option. Across nearly 10 years of AGMARKNET mirror records (42.72 million raw rows streamed, 2017–2026, spot-checked on one day at 100% Min/Max parity against official `data.gov.in` XML) and untouched 2025–2026 holdout data (`N = 69,526`), `MandiGuard` outperforms naive portals by +₹102.78/qtl (+₹3,083.5 per 30-quintal smallholder truckload), lowers dispatched loss-trip rate from 40.46% to 9.97%, lowers worst-10% tail loss from -₹436.67 (`B2`) to -₹37.89/qtl, and matches upfront all-tool profit using 44.4% fewer calls by design (8.75 vs 15.74). Even on the fair matched cohort where every top-5 candidate yard reports arrival-day prices (`N = 10,162`, zero simulated trap rules), `MandiGuard` still beats naive chasing by +₹38.38/qtl (₹510.89 vs ₹472.51/qtl) while lowering loss trips from 19.84% to 6.82%."*

## 2. The 5-Minute Synopsis Presentation

* **Slide 1 — The Problem (~45 sec):**  
  Most Indian mandi AI projects stop at predicting price MSE. Yet according to NSO *NSS Report No. 587*, smallholders harvest 29.33–38.73 quintals (~30-qtl truckload) while earning ₹3,798/month in average net crop income (50.2% indebted). When a portal shows a ₹1,330.49/qtl average cross-mandi spread, chasing it blindly loses money on 40.46% of dispatches, with worst-10% losses reaching -₹1,062.66/qtl (-₹31,879.8 on one load).

* **Slide 2 — 10-Year Multi-Modal Data & Official Anchors (~60 sec):**  
  We streamed 42.72 million AGMARKNET archive-mirror rows across 2017–2026 (yielding 883,406 rows in 178 2024-ranked core mandis, including 260,043 in 2024–2026), spot-checked on one day (`2023-10-18`, 172 matched keys out of 6,434 all-India rows) against official `data.gov.in` Resource `9ef84268-d588-465a-a308-a864a43d0070` XML (100.00% Min/Max parity, 99.42% Modal parity) alongside 22 live Nov-2026 `agmarknet.gov.in` queries (11/22 non-reporting over the prior 7 days). We added 4,427 grade-explicit HP Apple rows, 8,147 OSRM intra-state corridors, 291,384 NASA POWER rows (2017–2023) + 141,955 ERA5/NWP rows (2024–2026), and official GoI anchors: NABCONS (2022) market losses (Tomato 3.25%, Onion 1.96%, Apple 1.64%, Potato 0.86%), GoI APMC state fees (0.5% KA/GJ/HP-veg, 1.0% MH/HP-Apple, 1.5% MP/UP), and GoHP Economic Survey 2025–26 (91.59% single-lane hill roads).

* **Slide 3 — `MandiGuard` Four-Step Verification Cascade (~60 sec):**  
  We compare every system on four plain metrics: Mean Net Gain (₹/qtl), Loss-Trip Rate (%), Worst-10% Loss (₹/qtl), and Live API Calls/Query. `MandiGuard` runs cheapest-first `T1` → `T2` → `T3` → `T4` (with a validation-frozen ₹50/qtl LCB hurdle → `DISPATCH` or `REFUSE_PHANTOM_SPREAD`). It matches unconstrained all-tool utility (₹452.79/qtl) using 44.4% fewer calls than `B4` (8.75 vs 15.74)—an efficiency gain that follows by design from cheap-first ordering, with the empirical payoff appearing under a strict `B = 6` call budget (+₹104.54/qtl over `B4`, ₹403.85 vs ₹299.31/qtl, `Exp 5`).

* **Slide 4 — Confirmatory Results, Tradeoffs & Dual-View Open-Yard Defense (~90 sec):**  
  Across 69,526 holdout episodes (`R = 5,000` Two-Way Cluster Bootstrap, Holm `m = 4`): `H1` (+₹102.78/qtl over `B1`, p_Holm = 0.0012), `H3` (-37.29 pp episode losses vs `B3`, p_Holm = 0.0008), and `H4` (44.4% fewer calls vs `B4`, p_Holm = 0.0008) are supported. On `H2`, we disclose clearly that `B2` ties `MandiGuard` on overall mean profit (₹446.66 vs ₹452.79/qtl, p_Holm = 0.27); `MandiGuard` differs by halving loss trips (9.97% vs 18.11%) and limiting worst-10% tail loss (-₹37.89 vs -₹436.67/qtl), while the 4-state native-NWP check (+₹31.82/qtl) is exploratory context only. Similarly, on HP Apple 2026 (`Exp 3`), `B2` beats uncalibrated 2024 `MandiGuard` on overall mean (₹1,150.83 vs ₹605.06/qtl) while `MandiGuard` lowers traps (94.14% → 13.24%) and worst-10% loss (-₹8,487.24 → -₹102.76/qtl). Across 661,192 historical 2017–2026 episodes (`Exp 10`), loss-trip rate stays 8.78%–12.03% vs 39.18%–44.29% (`B1`).  
  On missing-data sensitivity (`Exp 15`), we show two complementary views: (1) on the `N = 69,526` crossover curve, `MandiGuard` leads `B1` through 0.5× freight / 5% discount (+₹42.43/qtl) and crosses below only at the zero-cost 0.0× / 0% boundary (-₹10.00/qtl, while keeping LTR 6.09% vs 18.52%); (2) on the fair matched `Observed-d0` cohort (`N = 10,162` identical episodes across all 4 policies, zero simulated traps), `MandiGuard` (₹510.89, 6.82% LTR, -₹36.07 CVaR10) beats `B1` (₹472.51, +₹38.38) and `B2` (₹469.99, +₹40.91), while point-ML `B3` earns +₹42.33/qtl higher mean (₹553.22) at 2.2× higher loss-trip rate (14.77%) and 10× deeper tail loss (-₹362.40). *(Exploratory Appendix illustrations `Exp 11`–`Exp 13b`—not main contributions—illustrate `Direct_Quantile_GBDT_Q20` at ₹459.02/qtl, `MandiGuard_ACI` trimming HP Apple tail loss by 27.6%, `ZeroCloud` at 3.84–5.53 calls, and 90-qtl FPO pooling at ₹477.28/qtl.)*

* **Slide 5 — Conclusion & Honest Scope (~15 sec):**  
  Reproducible, leak-checked, `.gov.in` spot-checked, and Colab-runnable under 3 minutes (4.82 MB)—moving mandi decision support from unguarded price chasing to risk-gated spatial dispatch, while noting clearly that freight, Q10 rot, and diversion penalties await live field validation and our 19/19 `pytest` checks verify code reproducibility and leakage guards rather than field economics.
