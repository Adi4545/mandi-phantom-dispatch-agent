Card 1 — Robert Jensen (2007), “The Digital Provide: Information (Technology), Market Performance, and Welfare in the South Indian Fisheries Sector”
The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 122(3):879–924 (2007) · DOI: 10.1162/qjec.122.3.879 · https://doi.org/10.1162/qjec.122.3.879

Question Asked: Does real-time market price information cut price dispersion, prevent waste, and raise producer and consumer welfare?

Setting & Data: Micro-surveys across 15 coastal beach fish markets in northern Kerala (1997–2001) during staggered mobile tower rollouts.

Method Explained Simply (Class 10–12): Before phones arrived, sardine boats returned blindly to home beaches, dumping unsold fish when local buyers were saturated while nearby ports went empty. Jensen compared early-coverage vs late-coverage beaches via difference-in-differences once boats could call ports from sea.

Key Verified Findings: Price dispersion fell sharply toward the Law of One Price, dumped catch fell to near zero, fishermen’s profits rose ~8%, and consumer prices fell ~4%.

Limitations / Gap Left Open: Coastal sardines were uniform over short open-water runs between active ports; inland mandis face variety splits, rotating closures, hill highways, and transit heat decay.

How MandiGuard Uses & Differs From It: Jensen sets our spatial-arbitrage baseline, while Exp 1 proves unverified inland screen chasing loses 40.46% of trips unless gated by T1–T4.

2-Sentence Synopsis Hook “Jensen (2007) proved mobile calls cut waste and raised Kerala fishermen’s profits ~8% for uniform catch over water. On inland mandis, variety splits, closed gates, freight, and heat turn raw screen chasing into a 40.46% loss rate.”
Q1. Why didn’t AGMARKNET phones recreate Jensen’s result for vegetables?
Screen quotes do not ensure open arrival auctions for exact varieties 1–3 days later, nor deduct highway freight, fees, and rot.
Q2. Does your thesis contradict Jensen (2007)?
No; on verified open yards (N=10,162, Exp 15), spatial dispatch yields ₹533.75/qtl under MandiGuard-CRC.
