Part 1: Foundations, Data Funnel & Causal Firewall

Abstract

Farmers and farmer producer organizations (FPOs) routinely compare local harvest prices against wholesale prices posted on government portals such as AGMARKNET before hiring a truck. On a portal screen, a ₹500 per quintal price difference between an origin mandi and a neighboring yard looks like immediate cash. It is not. This paper audits Indian daily wholesale records for onion, potato, tomato, and apple across 178 core markets (plus 42 graded-apple yards) in six states from 1 January 2024 to 1 September 2026 under a strict chronological timing firewall (<= t - 1) and a realistic sequential day-by-day arrival settlement model.

Across 58,676 lagged test episodes (1 June 2025 to 8 April 2026) showing a raw <= t - 1 price gap above ₹100 per quintal, naive screen-chasing (B1_lagged_screen, 100.00% dispatch) loses money on 45.21% of trips (mean ₹397.87/qtl, exact worst-10% expected shortfall ES10 = −₹641.26/qtl). Conditioning on sequential portal arrivals is crucial: under baseline missing-quote return assumptions (div = 1.5), 28.98% (17,005 trips) find zero commodity trading recorded on arrival and turn back; scoring the identical trips with 3-day hindsight drops apparent loss incidence to 31.35%.

Building up from fair age-matched road baselines (B2_age_matched, lag 1..3) and pre-registered tuned reliability-weighted abstention (RR_Abstain_matched, κ* = 5.0), we discover an even simpler, zero-hyperparameter rule (Safe_EV_Sunk_m0): multiply the 1-standard-deviation conservative spread (G1SD) by the yard's historical arrival-weekday reporting frequency (pdow), and dispatch iff pdow G1SD - Froad > 0 (equivalently, safe spread-to-freight ratio G1SD / Froad > 1 / pdow). Without tuning ANY threshold or κ grid, Safe_EV_Sunk_m0 (63.27% dispatch) achieves mean ₹465.59/qtl (+₹32.03 vs B2_age_matched), cuts episode loss incidence to 14.93% (−11.79 pp, two-sided Holm p = 0.0040), and strengthens ES10 to −₹344.09/qtl—a +₹181.17/qtl tail improvement over B2_age_matched (p_holm_2s = 0.0040), +₹168.69/qtl over hard-gated MG_matched, and +₹116.63/qtl over tuned RR_Abstain_matched across all three spatiotemporal clustering schemes (170–173 market clusters × 23 fortnights) and every single state and crop including graded apples (−₹1,454.86 vs −₹2,392.12 on HP apples). A stripped 4-function phone-calculator rule dropping volatility (RT_2F_Min_m0, pmin G > 2 Froad) raises mean net gain to ₹478.38/qtl (+₹44.82 vs B2, p_holm_2s = 0.0040) with zero free parameters. Across strict counterfactual tests, Safe_EV_Sunk_m0 maintains strong ES10 and loss reductions even when diversion penalties vanish (exact_zero_divert_payoff_0: +₹169.79/qtl ES10, p_2s = 0.002) and under open-yard Missing-At-Random portal omissions (+₹190.00/qtl ES10), though B2 earns higher mean payoff (+₹102.58/qtl) when blank portal rows represent unrecorded open sales—establishing that algorithmic triage helps FPOs filter screen noise prior to live trader confirmation, while not replacing phone verification or field trials. All results remain exploratory wrt prior calendar exposure (make reproduce, 0 untraceable numbers).

1. Three Research Questions and Honest Methodological Scope

Indian wholesale agricultural markets (mandis) publish daily minimum, modal, and maximum prices by variety on AGMARKNET. A widely held premise in agricultural informatics is that surfacing remote portal prices unlocks spatial arbitrage for farmers [jensen2007digital, goyal2010information]. In practice, four operational barriers stand between an online spread and cash in hand:

  1. Decision-Time Reporting Lag: At 6 a.m. dispatch time t—when FPO managers decide whether to load a truck or suppress an alert—same-day auction prices are not settled, and thin yards publish intermittently (1..7 days late).
  2. Transfer, Statutory Destination Fees, and Spoilage: Road freight, hill terrain, out-of-district APMC seller fees levied on destination auction value, and ambient transit decay shrink paper spreads [baulch1997transfer, nabcons2022phl].
  3. Price Dispersion and Arrival Execution Risk: A single high lagged modal quote on a volatile yard (dest_price_cv_28d) frequently reverts toward mean on arrival day t + τ, and an empty arrival board (settle_any_min_dw = NaN) means no transaction record exists in the portal [deka2026missing].
  4. Role of Automated Triage vs Live Phone Calls: Farmers hauling a 30-quintal truck typically confirm live yard openings via a 30-second phone call to a destination arhtiya (commission agent) [jensen2007digital]. Automated dispatch scoring serves as a 6 a.m. alert-triage filter preventing FPO managers from chasing hundreds of noisy portal spreads before dialing traders.

We ask three focused questions across six Indian states (Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh) and four crops (Onion, Potato, Tomato, Apple):

  • RQ1 (Screen-Chasing Loss Incidence): Under a <= t - 1 price firewall and non-anticipative sequential arrival settlement, how often do attractive lagged price spreads (> ₹100/qtl) lose money, and how severely do 3-day hindsight settlement and ex-post day-t origin conditioning distort perceived performance?
  • RQ2 (Can Zero-Parameter Expected-Value Rules Beat Both Fair Baselines and Tuned Heuristics?): On equal candidate menus and equal quote ages (1..3 days), can threshold-free, zero-hyperparameter rules (Safe_EV_Sunk_m0 and 4-function calculator RT_2F_Min_m0) improve episode loss incidence and worst-10% ES10 compared with age-matched road rules (B2_age_matched), hard-gated heuristics (MG_matched), and our tuned kappa*=5.0 extension (RR_Abstain)?
  • RQ3 (Identification Bounds, Subgroup Generalizability, and Structural Robustness): How sensitive are conclusions across full Manski/MAR missing-quote regimes (open_yard_MAR_lagged_price, exact_zero_divert_payoff_0, div = 0..2), frozen vs re-scored freight/commission tornados, exact-geocoded corridors vs district centroids, unconditional origin panels restoring unreported day-t origin rows, all six states, individual crops, and high-dispersion Himachal Pradesh graded apples?

2.3 Causal Timing Firewall and Feature Availability

At morning decision time t, every feature entering scoring or shortlisting predates day t (dest_quote_date < date, origin_quote_date < date). Realized settle_local_price_d0 and arrival columns settle_dest_modal_d0..d2, settle_any_min_d0..d2 exist exclusively for payoff settlement. tests/test_timing_and_leakage_r3.py corrupts all prices on days ≥ t* (inserting ₹99,999/qtl at excluded yards, halving day-t* local prices, and deleting active candidates on days ≥ t*) and asserts bit-for-bit equality of the candidate menu and all decision features on t ≤ t*.

3. Payoff Physics, Sequential Settlement, and Decision Rules (With Numeric Examples)

3.1 Physical Freight, Statutory Destination Fee (M9), and Spoilage

For corridor c = (o → d) in state s and commodity k:

Freightroad(c) = 35.0 + 0.45 · hs · road_km(c)

where hs = 1.45 in Himachal Pradesh (91.59% single-lane roads) and 1.0 elsewhere. Reference 20°C / 65% RH biological decay over transit truck_hours plus waiting days w ∈ {0, 1, 2} [kader2002postharvest, gross2016usda66] is:

s(k, w) = min(0.55, bk · max(1, truck_hours + 24 w)24)

with NABCONS 24h rates bk ∈ {0.0325 Tomato, 0.0196 Onion, 0.0164 Apple, 0.0086 Potato} [nabcons2022phl]. Statutory APMC seller fees τfee(c) ∈ {0, 0.005, 0.010, 0.015} (0 if same_district) apply strictly to post-spoilage destination gross sales, and equal 0 if the truck returns home unsold.

Worked Example 1 (Physical Freight, Spoilage & Destination-Base Statutory Fee): Consider an out-of-district Tomato trip in Madhya Pradesh (fee_rate = 1.5%, hs = 1.0) with road_km = 200 km, truck_hours = 6 hours, and immediate arrival sale (w = 0) at sale_price = ₹2,000/qtl, vs local settle_local_price_d0 = ₹1,500/qtl:

  1. Freight: F = 35.0 + 0.45 × 1.0 × 200 = ₹125.00/qtl.
  2. 20°C spoilage: s(0) = 0.0325 × (6 / 24) = 0.008125 (0.8125%).
  3. Post-spoilage gross auction revenue: ₹2,000 × (1 - 0.008125) = ₹1,983.75/qtl.
  4. Net after 1.5% statutory fee: ₹1,983.75 × 0.985 = ₹1,953.99/qtl.
  5. Modeled net dispatch gain: ₹1,953.99 - ₹125.00 - ₹1,500.00 = +₹328.99/qtl.

3.2 Sequential Non-Anticipative Settlement vs Hindsight Lookahead

Starting at w = 0 on arrival day tarr = t + τ (tau_days in 1..3), settlement advances day by day without future knowledge:

  • Sell exact: If exact-variety Modal_Price is reported on day w, sell: ΔNetsell(w) = Psale(1 - s(k, w))(1 - τfee) - F - 25 w - Ploc, d0.
  • Wait (w < 2): If exact variety is missing on day w but another variety of the crop trades (settle_any_min_dw > 0), wait 1 day (₹25/qtl-day detention + 24h decay).
  • Turn back / distress fallback: If no variety of the commodity is recorded on day w, divert home with zero APMC fee: ΔNetdivert(w) = -div· F - Ploc, d0· s(k, w) - 25 w (baseline div = 1.5; if day w = 2 still shows another variety, sell at 0.90 * settle_any_min_d2 after fee and ₹50 detention).

By contrast, 3-day hindsight settlement peeks ahead across d0..d2 even when day 0 shows zero commodity trade.

Worked Example 2 (Sequential No-Lookahead vs 3-Day Hindsight): On our MP Tomato trip (F = ₹125, Ploc, d0 = ₹1,500), suppose destination Tomato is completely absent on arrival day 0 (settle_any_min_d0 = NaN), though exact-variety trades at ₹2,000 on day +1:

  • Sequential (realistic): Seeing an empty tomato auction on day 0, the truck diverts at w = 0 (zero fee): ΔNet = -1.5 × 125 - 1,500 × 0.008125 = -₹199.69/qtl.
  • 3-day hindsight: Clairvoyant waiting (w = 1, s(1) = 0.040625, detention ₹25) records +₹239.97/qtl—a ₹439.66/qtl hindsight distortion.
