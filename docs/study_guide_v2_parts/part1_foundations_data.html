Part 1: Problem, Epistemic Table 1, Geocode Audit & Settlement Physics

1. Three Research Questions and Honest Methodological Scope

Indian wholesale agricultural markets (mandis) publish daily minimum, modal, and maximum prices by variety on AGMARKNET. While digital price dissemination reduces spoilage when execution is smooth (Jensen, 2007; Goyal, 2010), and targeted market-information services can raise realized farmer prices under symmetric bargaining (Courtois & Subervie, 2015), empirical trials across rural India show that portal quotes frequently fail to deliver arbitrage when transport, fees, volatility, and bargaining frictions bind (Fafchamps & Minten, 2012; Mitra et al., 2018; Chatterjee, 2023). Four operational mechanisms drive our retrospective audit:

  1. Reporting Latency (<= t - 1 or <= t - 2): At 6 a.m. decision day t, same-day auctions have not cleared, and thinner yards publish 1–7 days late (and even slower under multi-day portal lag).
  2. Transfer Frictions and Deterioration: Road freight, single-lane terrain, out-of-district market friction, and ambient transit decay erode nominal spreads (Baulch, 1997; NABCONS, 2022).
  3. Price Volatility and Silent Arrival Boards: Volatile lagged modal prices (dest_price_cv_28d) revert on arrival day t + τ, while blank arrival rows (settle_any_min_dw = NaN) trigger sequential turn-back or fallback losses (Deka, 2026).
  4. Role as an Upstream 6 a.m. Call-Priority Alert Filter: Smallholders routinely phone a destination arhtiya (commission agent) for 30 seconds before dispatch (Jensen, 2007; Ziegler et al., 2019). These heuristics filter 6 a.m. portal alerts to prioritize trader calls (PhoneCall_TopK_Open) and never replace live confirmation.

We examine six Indian states (Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh) and four crops (Onion, Potato, Tomato, Apple) through three questions:

  • RQ1 (Screen-Chasing Risk & Settlement Realism): Under a <= t - 1 causal firewall and sequential arrival settlement, how often do lagged portal spreads (> ₹100/qtl) lose money, and how strongly do 3-day hindsight and day-t origin conditioning distort apparent risk?
  • RQ2 (2×2 Risk Decomposition & Honest ML/OR Frontier): On equal Top-5 menus (age 1..3), how do volatility truncation (cv28) and reporting reliability (p_dow) decompose risk inside zero-tuned-parameter rules (Safe_EV_Sunk_m0, Safe_EV_Phys_m0, RT_2F_Min_m0) relative to full-season 17-month ML (Mean-Q10, heteroscedastic Split-CQR), OR baselines, and DS ∩ DB common-intersection routing vs screening?
  • RQ3 (Partial Identification, Scale Non-Invariance & Structural Stress Tests): How sensitive are conclusions to exact coupled Manski cond_ES10 bounds (Manski, 1990; Rubin, 1976), <= t - 2 portal latency, Schedule #7 biological decay, stochastic conditional MAR (Stoch_CondMAR), SUTVA crowding, non-collided geocodes, outsider Min_Price/LCV/4% arhtiya stress, B = 12 CGM G* inference, reverse-survivorship-aware 2018–2023 data (Sparks, 2018), and Himachal graded-apple scale non-invariance?

3. Payoff Physics, Sequential Settlement, and Decision Rules

3.1 Physical Freight, Stylized Fee Tiers (M9), and Biological Decay

For corridor c = (o → d) in state s and commodity k, freight is Froad(c) = 35.0 + 0.45 · hs · road_km(c) (h_s = 1.45 HP, 1.0 elsewhere). Ambient 20°C / 65% RH reference spoilage over transit truck_hours plus wait days w ∈ {0, 1, 2} (Kader, 2002; Gross et al., 2016) uses:

s(k, w) = min(0.55, bk · max(1, truck_hours + 24 w)24)

with stylized daily rates bk ∈ {0.0325 Tomato, 0.0196 Onion, 0.0164 Apple, 0.0086 Potato} calibrated from NABCONS cumulative shares (NABCONS, 2022) — contrasted directly in v7_exp2 against biological Schedule #7 (24τdays from harvest/loading + USDA-66 Q10 = 2(Tmax-20)/10). Stylized destination fees τfee ∈ {0, 0.005, 0.010, 0.015} apply strictly on post-decay destination gross auction sales (0 intra-district or unsold return).

Worked Example 1 (Physical Freight, Decay & Post-Decay Fee): On an out-of-district MP Tomato trip (fee = 1.5%, km = 200, truck_hours = 6, arrival sale ₹2,000 vs local ₹1,500): F = 35 + 0.45(200) = ₹125.00/qtl; s(0) = 0.0325(6/24) = 0.008125; post-decay gross = 2000(0.991875) = ₹1,983.75; net after 1.5% fee = 1983.75(0.985) = ₹1,953.99; net gain = 1953.99 - 125 - 1500 = +₹328.99/qtl.

3.2 Sequential Non-Anticipative Settlement vs Hindsight Lookahead

Starting at w = 0 on arrival t + τ (tau_days in 1..3): if exact variety trades (Modal_Price present), sell at Psale(1 - s(k, w))(1 - τfee) - F - 25w - Ploc, d0; if missing while another variety trades (settle_any_min_dw > 0), wait 1 day (₹25 + decay) for w < 2 (0.90 * settle_any_min_d2 at w = 2); if zero commodity trade prints on day w, divert home: ΔNetdivert(w) = -div· F - Ploc, d0 s(k, w) - 25w (div = 1.5, zero fee). Crucially, Revision v8 fixes the _spoil_return_leg bug in S6_Smallholder_4pctArh_USDA_ERA5_Frozen so unsold return legs accumulate full 24τdays + hrs + 24w biological clock and full realized ERA5 multipliers (q10, rh_f, chill, rain_f). Blindly waiting 1–2 days regardless of empty day-0 boards is tested in Causal_BlindWait (v7_exp2).

Worked Example 2 (Sequential vs 3-Day Hindsight): If destination Tomato is absent on arrival day 0 yet trades at ₹2,000 on day +1, sequential settlement returns at w = 0 (ΔNet = -1.5(125) - 1500(0.008125) = -₹199.69), whereas 3-day hindsight records +₹239.97 — a ₹439.66/qtl distortion.
