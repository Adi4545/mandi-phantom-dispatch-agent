Part 1: Problem, Epistemic Table 1, Data Provenance & Settlement Physics

1. Three Research Questions and Honest Methodological Scope

Indian wholesale agricultural markets (mandis) publish daily minimum, modal, and maximum prices by variety on AGMARKNET. While digital price dissemination can narrow spatial dispersion when market frictions are low [jensen2007digital, goyal2010information], empirical evidence across rural India and developing economies shows that raw price portals frequently fail to translate into realized arbitrage [fafchamps2012sms, courtois2015farmer, mitra2018asymmetric, chatterjee2023market]. Four operational mechanisms separate a screen spread from realized net gain:

  1. Decision-Time Reporting Lag: At 6 a.m. dispatch time t—when FPO managers decide whether to load a truck or suppress a portal alert—same-day auction prices have not cleared, and thinner yards publish intermittently (1..7 days late).
  2. Transfer Costs, Destination Statutory Fees, and Biological Decay: Road freight, single-lane hill terrain, out-of-district APMC seller fees levied on destination auction value, and ambient transit spoilage erode gross price gaps [baulch1997transfer, nabcons2022phl].
  3. Auction Volatility and Arrival Execution Risk: A single elevated lagged modal quote at a volatile market (dest_price_cv_28d) frequently reverts by arrival day t + τ, and an empty arrival board (settle_any_min_dw = NaN) indicates no recorded commodity trade in the public portal [deka2026missing].
  4. Role of Automated Triage vs Live Trader Confirmation: Farmers dispatching a 30-quintal load normally verify live yard activity through a 30-second phone call to a destination arhtiya (commission agent) [jensen2007digital, ziegler2019fresh]. Automated dispatch scoring operates strictly as an upstream 6 a.m. alert-triage filter helping FPO coordinators prioritize promising corridors and suppress noisy alerts before dialing traders, rather than replacing phone verification.

We address three research questions across six Indian states (Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh) and four crops (Onion, Potato, Tomato, Apple):

  • RQ1 (Screen-Chasing Loss Incidence and Settlement Realism): Under a strict <= t - 1 chronological firewall and non-anticipative sequential arrival settlement, how often do attractive lagged portal spreads (> ₹100/qtl) lose money, and how strongly do 3-day hindsight settlement and ex-post day-t origin conditioning distort observed risk?
  • RQ2 (Zero-Parameter Expected-Value Rules vs Equal-Age Baselines and Tuned Heuristics): On identical Top-5 candidate menus and equal quote ages (1..3 days), can threshold-free, zero-hyperparameter rules (Safe_EV_Sunk_m0, settlement-matched Safe_EV_Phys_m0, and Class-8 phone-calculator RT_2F_Min_m0) improve episode loss incidence and worst-10% ES10 over validation-tuned road rules (B2_age_matched), hard-gated heuristics (MG_matched), and our tuned reliability-weighted extension (RR_Abstain_matched, κ* = 5.0)?
  • RQ3 (Identification Bounds, Zero-Lookahead Panel, Realized ERA5 Weather, and Subgroup Generalizability): How stable are these gains across exact silent-market break-even thresholds and Manski/MAR missing-quote bounds [rubin1976inference, manski1990bounds], a fresh zero-lookahead 2024-only 171-market rebuild, realized ERA5 destination arrival-day weather settlement, 18 frozen and re-scored cost scenarios, exact-geocoded corridors, an 88,014-episode unconditional origin panel, all six states, every crop, and high-dispersion Himachal Pradesh graded apples?

2.4 Causal Timing Firewall and Counterfactual Property Tests

At morning decision time t, every input entering shortlisting or policy scoring predates day t (dest_quote_date < date, origin_quote_date < date). Realized settle_local_price_d0, arrival quotes settle_dest_modal_d0..d2, settle_any_min_d0..d2, and Section 4.4 realized ERA5 weather columns reside exclusively in the settlement namespace. Automated counterfactual tests in tests/test_timing_and_leakage_r3.py corrupt all prices on days ≥ t* (injecting ₹99,999/qtl spikes at excluded yards, halving day-t* local prices, and deleting active candidates on days ≥ t*) and assert bit-for-bit equality of the shortlisted candidate menu and every decision feature for t ≤ t*.

3. Payoff Physics, Sequential Settlement, and Decision Rules (With Numeric Examples)

3.1 Physical Freight, Statutory Destination Fee (M9), and Biological Spoilage

For corridor c = (o → d) in state s and commodity k:

Freightroad(c) = 35.0 + 0.45 · hs · road_km(c)

where hs = 1.45 in Himachal Pradesh (91.59% single-lane roads [hpeconsurvey2026]) and 1.0 elsewhere. Reference 20°C / 65% RH biological decay over transit truck_hours plus waiting days w ∈ {0, 1, 2} [kader2002postharvest, gross2016usda66] is:

s(k, w) = min(0.55, bk · max(1, truck_hours + 24 w)24)

with NABCONS 24-hour market-level rates bk ∈ {0.0325 Tomato, 0.0196 Onion, 0.0164 Apple, 0.0086 Potato} [nabcons2022phl]. In our Section 4.4 realized ERA5 settlement check, decay scales by ambient arrival-day Q10 = 2(Tmax-20)/10 and humidity factor 1 + 0.25 max(0, (RH-70)/30), while heavy arrival rain (rain >= 35 mm) adds +0.02 rot fraction and shifts realized exact-variety sale price halfway toward Min_Price. Statutory APMC seller fees τfee(c) ∈ {0, 0.005, 0.010, 0.015} (0 if same_district) apply strictly to post-spoilage destination gross sales, and equal 0 if the truck returns home unsold.

Worked Example 1 (Physical Freight, Spoilage & Destination-Base Statutory Fee): Consider an out-of-district Tomato trip in Madhya Pradesh (fee_rate = 1.5%, hs = 1.0) with road_km = 200 km, truck_hours = 6 hours, and immediate arrival sale (w = 0) at sale_price = ₹2,000/qtl, vs local settle_local_price_d0 = ₹1,500/qtl:

  1. Freight: F = 35.0 + 0.45 × 1.0 × 200 = ₹125.00/qtl.
  2. 20°C spoilage: s(0) = 0.0325 × (6 / 24) = 0.008125 (0.8125%).
  3. Post-spoilage gross auction revenue: ₹2,000 × (1 - 0.008125) = ₹1,983.75/qtl.
  4. Net after 1.5% statutory fee: ₹1,983.75 × 0.985 = ₹1,953.99/qtl.
  5. Modeled net dispatch gain: ₹1,953.99 - ₹125.00 - ₹1,500.00 = +₹328.99/qtl.

3.2 Sequential Non-Anticipative Settlement vs Hindsight Lookahead

Starting at w = 0 on arrival day tarr = t + τ (tau_days in 1..3), settlement advances day by day without future lookahead:

  • Sell exact: If exact-variety Modal_Price is reported on day w, sell: ΔNetsell(w) = Psale(1 - s(k, w))(1 - τfee) - F - 25 w - Ploc, d0.
  • Wait (w < 2): If exact variety is missing on day w while another variety of the crop trades (settle_any_min_dw > 0), wait 1 day (₹25/qtl-day detention + 24h decay).
  • Turn back / distress fallback: If no variety of the commodity is recorded on day w, divert home with zero APMC fee: ΔNetdivert(w) = -div· F - Ploc, d0· s(k, w) - 25 w (baseline div = 1.5; if day w = 2 still shows another variety, sell at 0.90 * settle_any_min_d2 after fee and ₹50 detention).

By contrast, 3-day hindsight settlement peeks ahead across d0..d2 even when arrival day 0 shows zero commodity trade.

Worked Example 2 (Sequential No-Lookahead vs 3-Day Hindsight): On our MP Tomato trip (F = ₹125, Ploc, d0 = ₹1,500), suppose destination Tomato is absent on arrival day 0 (settle_any_min_d0 = NaN), though exact-variety trades at ₹2,000 on day +1:

  • Sequential (realistic): Seeing an empty tomato auction on day 0, the truck diverts at w = 0 (zero fee): ΔNet = -1.5 × 125 - 1,500 × 0.008125 = -₹199.69/qtl.
  • 3-day hindsight: Clairvoyant waiting (w = 1, s(1) = 0.040625, detention ₹25) records +₹239.97/qtl—a ₹439.66/qtl hindsight distortion.
